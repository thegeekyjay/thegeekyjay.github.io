Masters Golf Math — Algorithm Backtest 2015–2024

Applying Hannah VanBiber's 10-filter algorithm (The Athletic, 2025) retroactively to the past 10 Masters tournaments.

The 10 Filters

Filter 1
Not a Masters debutant — no first-time player has won since 1979.
Filter 2
Top 25 in Data Golf / world rankings at time of tournament.
OWGR used as proxy for pre-2020 years (Data Golf not retroactively available).
Filter 3
Previous top-30 finish at Augusta (26 of last 28 winners).
Filter 4
Made the cut at Augusta the year before winning (since 1998).
Filter 5
Not the pre-tournament betting favourite (only 1 favourite won since 2006).
Filter 6
Not the defending champion (no back-to-back since Tiger 2001–02).
Filter 7
Top 10 in at least one of the previous 3 starts before Augusta (9 of 10 winners).
Filter 8
At least one previous top-10 finish at Augusta (8 of 10 winners).
Filter 9
Top 50 in driving distance the prior year (16 of 17 winners).
Filter 10
3 or more PGA Tour wins in career (13 of 14 winners).

Which Filters Failed Most?

Counts how many times each filter eliminated the eventual winner. F5 (not the favourite) is the single most problematic filter in this 10-year window.

Honest Assessment of the Algorithm

⚠ The Favourite Problem (F5) is the biggest flaw

Filter 5 eliminates the pre-tournament favourite. Scottie Scheffler won in both 2022 and 2024 as the clear favourite. That's 2 of the 5 misses caused by a single filter. The trend Hannah cites ("only 1 favourite since 2006") was technically true at time of recording — but it masks that Scheffler is a structural anomaly. He's #1 in the world by a wide margin; the historical trend was built on a more competitive era.

⚠ Retroactive application is partially circular

Some filters (F3, F4, F8 — Augusta history) are clean to apply retroactively. Others like F7 (recent form — top 10 in prior 3 starts) require knowing the exact tournament schedule and results week-by-week, which introduces uncertainty. The analysis above uses best available knowledge but F7 assessments carry more uncertainty than the others.

✓ What the algorithm does well

When it hits, it hits cleanly — 2015 Spieth, 2020 DJ, 2023 Rahm were all well-supported by the filters. The Augusta history filters (F3, F4, F8) are genuinely predictive. The algorithm is better used as a "plausibility screen" than a winner-picker — it correctly identifies the contention zone even when the exact winner slips through.

Bottom line: In a 10-year backtest, the algorithm correctly includes the winner in its finalist group roughly 3–4 times out of 10 (30–40%). Given it narrows 91 players to ~3–5 finalists, that's above random chance — but it's not a reliable predictor. The Scheffler era exposes its core weakness around dominant favourites.
